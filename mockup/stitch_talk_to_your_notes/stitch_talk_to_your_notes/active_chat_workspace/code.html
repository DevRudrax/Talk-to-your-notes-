Explain normalization and the difference between 2NF and 3NF.

temp_preferences_custom

Normalization is the process of organizing data in a database to reduce redundancy and improve data integrity. It involves dividing large tables into smaller, related tables and defining relationships between them based on specific rules (Normal Forms).

Key Differences: 2NF vs. 3NF

Feature 2nd Normal Form (2NF) 3rd Normal Form (3NF)
Prerequisite Must be in 1NF. Must be in 2NF.
Core Rule No partial dependency. All non-key attributes must depend on the entire primary key. No transitive dependency. Non-key attributes must not depend on other non-key attributes.
Focus Composite primary keys. Relationships between non-key columns.

In short: 2NF ensures data relates directly to the primary key, while 3NF ensures data doesn't relate indirectly through another non-key column.

Second Normal Form (2NF)

A relation is in 2NF if it is in 1NF and every non-prime attribute is fully functionally dependent on the primary key. This means no partial dependency exists.

Third Normal Form (3NF)

A relation is in 3NF if it is in 2NF and no non-prime attribute is transitively dependent on the primary key.
